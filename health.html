Core Framework & Principles

Founder of Attia Medical, host of The Drive, and author of Outlive (2023). Pioneers proactive prevention of chronic disease, with heavy emphasis on cardiovascular conditioning and strength training. His targets run more aggressive than standard guidelines, and he has commercial ties — read the numbers as his positions, not consensus.

Zone 2 Cardio

3+ hours a week of easy-moderate cardio, at a pace where you can still hold a conversation — his floor is 3 x 60 min or 4 x 45 min.

3+ hrs / week
VO2 Max Intervals

One hard interval session a week (e.g. 4 x 4 min work/rest) to push top-end fitness.

1x / week
Strength 2-3x / Week

Grip, pulling, hip hinge (deadlift), squatting, carrying heavy things — plus stability work as a fourth pillar.

2-3x / week
Protein ~1 g / lb

About 1 gram per pound of target body weight a day (2.2 g/kg), spread over ~4 meals. His stated minimum is 1.6 g/kg — well above the Dietary Guidelines' 1.2-1.6 g/kg.

1 g / lb target weight
Sleep 7-8 Hours

Cool, dark room on a consistent schedule. Sleep is non-negotiable for recovery and metabolic health.

7-8 hrs
Know Your Numbers

ApoB and Lp(a) once, fasting glucose and HbA1c, blood pressure — track trends over time, not single readings.

ApoB · Lp(a) · HbA1c
Screen for Cancer Early

Age-appropriate screening (e.g. colonoscopy) — early detection is part of the prevention model, not an afterthought.

Screen early
Centenarian Decathlon

Train now for the physical tasks you want to do at 100: carry groceries, get up off the floor, hike with grandchildren.

Train for 100

Core Framework & Principles

Professor of Neurobiology and Ophthalmology at Stanford School of Medicine, host of the Huberman Lab podcast, and author of Protocols (2026). Actionable protocols for sleep, focus, and physical performance — though some are stated more confidently than the evidence supports, and the podcast carries sponsors (AG1, Eight Sleep, LMNT) alongside the advice.

Morning Light

Outside within 30-60 min of waking: ~10 min on a clear day, ~20 min cloudy, 30-60 min overcast. No sunglasses; never stare at the sun.

10 min clear
Same Wake Time

Wake at the same time every day and go to bed when you first feel sleepy; hold it within ~30 min most nights.

Same time daily
Delay Caffeine 90-120 Min

Wait 90-120 min after waking for the first caffeine — his fix for the afternoon slump. No trial has tested the timing; treat it as a personal experiment.

90-120 min
Sleep Basics

Cool, dark room; dim lights and screens in the evening; no caffeine 8-10 hrs before bed; no alcohol — it fragments deep sleep and REM.

Cool · dark · no alcohol
Supplements Last

Fix light, timing, temperature, caffeine, and stress first. Only then, one compound at a time with a doctor: magnesium threonate or bisglycinate, apigenin, theanine.

Basics first
Deliberate Cold

~11 min of cold exposure per week total, water uncomfortably cold but safe — his protocol for resilience and recovery.

11 min / week
Deep Focus Blocks

Ultradian rhythm deep-work blocks of ~90 min, with real breaks between them.

90 min blocks
Train Across Modalities

Zone 2 cardio, resistance training, and high-intensity intervals across the week — strength and cardio both matter.

Zone 2 + lift + intervals

Core Framework & Principles

PhD in biomedical science (mitochondrial metabolism, St. Jude; postdoc with Bruce Ames on micronutrients). Founder of FoundMyFitness. Translates primary research on micronutrients, heat/cold stress (hormesis), and aging into practical protocols — and is explicit that her routine is personal, not a prescription.

Sauna / Heat Stress

4-7 sessions a week, ~20 min at ~174°F. Finnish cohort studies link this to ~40% lower all-cause mortality and ~66% lower dementia risk — observational, not proven causal. Hotter is not better: above ~190°F the benefit fades.

20 min, 174°F
Micronutrient Core

Sulforaphane (broccoli sprouts), omega-3 EPA/DHA, vitamin D3 — her three priorities.

Sulforaphane · EPA/DHA · D3
Omega-3, Tested

Aim for an omega-3 index of 8-12% (red-blood-cell test). She discusses ~2-4 g/day EPA+DHA — note trials show a dose-dependent atrial-fibrillation signal above ~1 g/day.

Index 8-12%
Vitamin D, Tested

Test 25(OH)D and supplement to her preferred 40-60 ng/mL — don't guess. Note the largest trial (VITAL) found no benefit from supplementing beyond correcting deficiency.

Test, don't guess
Exercise & VO2 Max

Regular exercise, with VO2 max the metric she calls the single most important for predicting lifespan.

VO2 max
Daily Multivitamin

A daily multivitamin: the COSMOS trial found about two years' slower global cognitive aging after two years of daily use.

COSMOS trial
APOE4 & DHA

If you carry APOE4, DHA reaches the brain less efficiently — she discusses higher doses (~2 g) in phospholipid form.

APOE4 · DHA

Core Framework & Principles

Cardiologist and geneticist at Scripps Research; author of Super Agers (2025). Studies the "wellderly" — people 80-105 with no chronic disease — and argues the basics beat the gadgets.

Exercise Comes First

The single most effective medical intervention known for healthy aging, in his words. His own routine: aerobic ~30 min a day, 5 days a week, plus resistance 2-3x a week.

30 min x 5 + lift
Strength, Not Just Cardio

He used to be cardio-only and changed his mind: strength training is now essential, and grip and leg strength are aging markers.

Grip strength
Mostly Plants

Mostly plants, Mediterranean-style, few ultra-processed foods. The pattern matters more than any single food.

Plants · Mediterranean
Know Your Numbers

Blood pressure, HbA1c, LDL/ApoB, and a one-time Lp(a) — the fundamentals, tracked over time.

BP · HbA1c · ApoB · Lp(a)
Sleep ~7 Hours

About 7 hours is the sweet spot in the data. The evidence for melatonin, magnesium, and temperature beds is underwhelming.

~7 hrs
People Matter

Purpose, a rich social life, nature, music, optimism — the wellderly were thinner, more active, and "remarkably upbeat." Loneliness is a hazard.

Social connection
Skeptical of the Sell

He takes no supplements himself. Biological-age tests: expensive, accuracy unclear. Full-body scans in healthy people: curious, not proven.

No supplements

Cardiologist and dean of the Friedman School at Tufts; directs the Food is Medicine Institute. Argues food quality — not calories alone — drives health, and that food belongs in healthcare.

Quality Over Calories

What you eat matters more than how much: nutrient-dense, minimally processed foods over calorie counting.

Quality first

Less UPF · sugar · salt

Core Framework & Principles

Metabolic scientist; senior investigator at the NIH for 21 years until his early retirement in April 2025, citing censorship of his ultra-processed-food research. Author of Food Intelligence (2025).

The Ultra-Processed Trial

In his 2019 NIH inpatient trial, people on an ultra-processed diet ate ~500 extra kcal a day and gained ~0.9 kg in two weeks — with nutrient-matched meals.

+500 kcal / day
Hyperpalatable Foods

A follow-up trial: a hyperpalatable, energy-dense ultra-processed diet led to ~1,000 extra kcal a day. The food environment drives it, not willpower.

+1,000 kcal / day
Build Around Whole Foods

Meals built from minimally processed foods; keep hyper-tasty snacks out of the house.

Whole foods first
Low-Carb: No Magic

At the same calories, low-carb has no special metabolic advantage for fat loss — the diet you can keep wins.

Calories equal
Maintenance Is Environment

Keeping weight off is about appetite and environment, not willpower — change the surroundings, not just the resolve.

Change the room
Exercise Still Helps

Exercise aids weight maintenance and health even when it doesn't move the scale much.

Core Framework & Principles

Nutrition scientist at Stanford; led the DIETFITS trial and the 2023 twin study on vegan vs omnivore diets. His message: the diet you can keep beats the "perfect" diet.

DIETFITS: No Winner

609 adults, 12 months: healthy low-fat lost 5.3 kg, healthy low-carb 6.0 kg — a 0.7 kg difference that was not statistically significant.

No winner
What Worked in Both

More vegetables, less added sugar, fewer refined grains, mostly whole foods, more home cooking — the shared pattern, not the label.

The shared pattern
No Predictor

Neither genotype nor baseline insulin predicted who did better on which diet — individual response varies widely.

No one-size-fits-all
Pick What You'll Keep

Ask "Could I eat this way for years?" Adherence and diet quality, not diet class, tracked success.

Years, not weeks
Plant-Heavy Lowers LDL Fast

In his 2023 twin study, 8 weeks of vegan eating cut LDL cholesterol by about 10% vs an omnivore diet.

LDL -10% in 8 wks
Quality Beats the Label

Even within a diet label, food quality decides the outcome — DIETFITS tested healthy versions of both diets, not junk ones.

Quality decides

Core Framework & Principles

Professor of kinesiology at McMaster University; led the meta-analyses that set modern protein targets for muscle. His message: lift, eat enough protein, and don't fear the weights as you age.

Lift 2-3x a Week

Major muscle groups, 2-3 times a week. Training near failure matters more than the load you use.

2-3x / week
Protein 1.2-1.6 g/kg

For most adults, 1.2-1.6 g/kg a day — the top of that range is where the benefit plateaus (his 2018 meta-analysis). Higher end if older, dieting, or training hard.

1.2-1.6 g/kg
Per Meal

About 25-40 g per meal (0.25 g/kg for young adults, 0.4 g/kg for older adults); ~2-3 g of leucine per meal switches muscle-building on.

25-40 g / meal
Food First

Whole food first; powders are convenient, not required.

Food first
Older Adults Need More

Muscle is lost faster with age and needs more protein plus strength work to hold on — anabolic resistance is real.

Age raises the bar
Creatine 3-5 g

3-5 g of creatine a day: effective for almost everyone who trains, in his words.

3-5 g / day
Break Up Inactivity

Long stretches without moving cost muscle fast — break them up, especially when dieting or ill.

Don't sit all day

All five

Core Framework & Principles

Circadian biologist at the Salk Institute; author of The Circadian Code (2018). His core idea: when you eat and sleep matters almost as much as what you eat.

Light in the Morning

Bright light outdoors within ~30 min of waking (15-30 min); dim the lights 2 hours before bed.

Light in the morning
Same Times Daily

Same wake and sleep times every day, weekends included — the clock runs on regularity.

Same times daily
Eat in a 10-12 Hr Window

Start with 12 hours, trim ~1 hour a week toward 8-11. A consistent 10-hour window 5 days a week beats an irregular 8-hour one.

10-12 hr window
Stop Eating Before Bed

Stop eating 2-3 hours before bed; late meals and bright screens both push sleep later.

2-3 hrs before bed
The Mouse Study

His 2012 mouse study: the same high-fat food eaten in an 8-hour window led to 28% less weight gain.

28% less gain (mice)
Human Trials Are Mixed

Small trials show benefits (firefighters on a 10-hour window improved blood lipids); bigger trials found no weight-loss difference. A tool, not a cure.

Mixed trials
No Heavy Late Meals

The body processes food worse when the clock says sleep — keep evenings light.

No late meals

Where They Agree: A Daily Checklist

Fifteen experts, different methods — but they converge on the same daily habits:

  1. Move daily; lift 2–3 times a week; add regular cardio.
  2. Eat mostly whole, minimally processed foods, lots of plants.
  3. Enough protein, spread across the day.
  4. Cut sugary drinks, ultra-processed snacks, and excess alcohol.
  5. Sleep 7–8 hours on a consistent schedule; get daylight in the morning.
  6. Don't smoke.
  7. Know your blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol (ApoB if possible).
  8. Stay socially connected.
  9. Be wary of expensive supplements and anyone selling certainty.

Not personal medical advice. Check with your doctor before changing exercise, diet, supplements, or medications.
