How to play

  1. Draw a track — drag on the canvas with your mouse or finger. Downhill slopes build speed; ramps launch him into the air. The 📏 Straight tool draws one straight line between two points — press, drag, let go.
  2. Three kinds of line — pick before you draw. Normal (blue) is the track he rides. Boost (red) speeds him up while he rides it — and if he hits a mushroom while he's on a red line, the two boosts stack and he goes extra fast. Scenery (green) is just for drawing the world around the track; he passes right through it.
  3. Move the view — tap ✋ Move, then drag to slide the window around without drawing anything. Zoom with the − / + buttons, scroll on the canvas, or pinch on your phone; "Fit track" shows the entire track at once. The view stays exactly where you leave it — it never snaps back.
  4. Press Play (or Space) — the rider sets off from the flag. While he's airborne, ← and → spin him. Land on your wheels, not your head.
  5. Don't crash — if his head hits a line, he's done. Pause (Space) to draw more track, then Play to keep going.
  6. Tools — Erase wipes lines, Clear all starts fresh, Default track brings back the starter track, Restart (R) sends him back to the flag. Drew a bad line? Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back the last stroke, Redo (Ctrl+Shift+Z) puts it back, and Backspace deletes the last line. The 🚩 Flag tool plants a checkpoint on the track — Restart then starts him there instead. Speed slows the ride to ½× or ¼× so you can watch a landing up close. Your best distance is saved on your device.

Fly through the real, mapped universe — every star and galaxy at its true position.

Keys: W A S D to fly, R and F to rise and sink, scroll to set speed. Drag to look around — the sky follows your finger, and a flick keeps it turning; tap a name to fly to it. On a phone, hold the Fly button and drag.

Every position is real: 99,692 stars from the HYG database (Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star and Gliese catalogs) and 10,124 galaxies from OpenNGC, built on NASA’s Extragalactic Database — both CC BY-SA 4.0. Named galaxies use published distances; the deep cloud’s distances come from redshift, so they are approximate. Stars and galaxies are drawn far larger than life — at true size they would be invisible points — and flight is faster than light. The speed readout says by how much. Sizes follow a logarithm — each symbol grows with the star's real brightness or the galaxy's real width, squeezed until everything in the catalog can be seen — while every position and distance stays exactly as measured. The sky's soft glow is summed from the catalog itself — where mapped stars crowd together, their light is added into a faint haze, so the texture you see is the catalog's own density, and no light appears where no stars are mapped. The brightest stars shimmer slightly, and left alone the view drifts very slowly. The Sun and planets hang at their true directions and relative distances for 18 November 2026, but their sizes are enlarged to be seen — the planets 1,000×, the Sun 40×; their sizes relative to each other stay true. Surfaces are published mission and reference maps (NASA/ESA mission mosaics and Solar System Scope maps, CC BY 4.0 / CC0 via Wikimedia Commons); Saturn's rings are drawn from the published ring profile, not a photograph. Earth's spin phase is not shown. The bright nebulae are real places from the same OpenNGC catalog — their directions and sizes are the catalog's, their distances are published values — drawn as soft coloured glows: pink-red where young stars light the gas, teal for planetary nebulae, violet for supernova remnants, blue for starlight reflected off dust; the wisps inside each glow are a drawing convention, like the galaxies' spiral arms. Bright objects bloom — light spilling around the brightest stars, nebulae and galaxy cores — the way bright things bloom to an eye; nothing blooms that is not bright in the data.

THUMB KART

Your kart steers itself around the corners — your thumbs do the driving.

RIGHT thumb — GO: hold to speed up, let go to brake. While you hold, slide ◀ ▶ to lean across the track. In a corner, let go and press again fast to drift — finish the move for a boost.

LEFT thumb — BANANA: drive over a bunch to pick one up. Hold to carry it behind you as a shield, let go to drop it, flick up to throw it ahead.
