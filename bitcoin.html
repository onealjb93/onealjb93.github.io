Bitcoin Power Law

Bitcoin's price has spent fifteen years oscillating around a single straight line. Plot price against time since the genesis block with both axes logarithmic, and the chaos of four boom-and-bust cycles averages out into a power law — price ≈ 10−16.493 × days5.688. Physicist Giovanni Santostasi formalised the model in 2018; the fit still explains about 96% of the variance in log price (R² ≈ 0.96).

How to read it

  • Below the trend — the model frames Bitcoin as undervalued relative to its fifteen-year path. Deep below trend, near the green floor, has historically been the accumulation zone.
  • Above the trend — the model frames it as stretched. Bull-market peaks have topped out lower each cycle relative to trend (about 11× in 2013, 6.6× in 2017, 3.6× in 2021, about 1.4× in 2024–25).
  • Where it stands now — spot sits below the trend line but just above the support floor. The model says how stretched price is, never when it will move.

Honest limitations

  • A fit to the past, not a law of nature. Fifteen years and roughly four cycles is a striking sample, not a guarantee. Regulation, a technical failure, or a better competitor could bend the curve.
  • The exponent is not a constant. It is a regression estimate that shifts as data arrives, and different starting dates give anything from about 4.9 to 5.9. Distant projections swing wildly with small changes.
  • Wide bands. "Within −60% to +150% of trend" is a huge range in the short term. This is a compass for years, not a trading signal for Tuesdays.
  • Not financial advice — one lens among many.
