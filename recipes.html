Yield: 4 servings | Health Focus: Gut Microbiome & Anti-Inflammatory Fats

Why It's Healthy: Red miso delivers a deep concentration of fermented probiotics and umami-rich amino acids supporting gut health, extra-virgin olive oil supplies stable monounsaturated fats, and Brussels sprouts provide high doses of sulforaphane.

Ingredients
  • ⅓ cup red miso & ⅓ cup maple syrup
  • ⅓ cup extra-virgin olive-oil & 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 12 oz Brussels sprouts (trimmed and halved)
  • 3 to 4 chicken breasts (~2 lbs, cubed into 1 ½-inch pieces)
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Heat oven to 425°F. Line a baking sheet with aluminum foil.
  2. Step 2: Whisk red miso, maple syrup, olive oil, and cumin in a bowl. Reserve a scant ¼ cup (3 tbsp) of mixture for later. Toss chicken and sprouts in the remaining marinade.
  3. Step 3: Spread everything in a single layer on the baking sheet, scraping remaining marinade on top.
  4. Step 4: Bake 8 mins. Remove, daub reserved 3 tbsp marinade on top, and bake 4 mins more.
  5. Step 5: Switch to broil for 1–2 mins until chicken is cooked through and slightly charred. Serve warm.
Substitutions: Chicken can be swapped for steak or salmon. Brussels sprouts can be swapped for green beans or mushrooms.
Yield: 4 to 6 servings | Health Focus: Cruciferous Phytonutrients & Healthy Sesame Lipids

Why It's Healthy: Raw Taiwanese flathead or Napa cabbage is rich in glucosinolates and Vitamin C, while toasted sesame oil and sesame seeds provide heart-healthy lignans and essential minerals.

Ingredients
  • 1 lb Taiwanese flathead cabbage or Napa cabbage
  • Kosher salt (Diamond Crystal) and black pepper
  • 2 medium garlic cloves (finely grated into paste)
  • 2 tbsp toasted sesame oil & 1 tbsp untoasted sesame seeds
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Trim outer leaves and remove the solid core from the cabbage.
  2. Step 2: Cut leaves into rough 1 ¼-inch squares; slice thick core-adjacent pieces into thin ¼-inch strips.
  3. Step 3: Wash, drain, and place cabbage in a large bowl. Sprinkle with 3 tbsp salt, scrunch with hands until mixed and beginning to soften, then let sit for 10 minutes.
  4. Step 4: Whisk garlic paste, sesame oil, ⅛ tsp salt, and ¼ tsp pepper in a small bowl.
  5. Step 5: Rinse salt off cabbage thoroughly under running water, drain well, and set aside.
  6. Step 6: Toss cabbage with dressing and sprinkle with sesame seeds right before serving. Best eaten fresh!
Tip: Taiwanese flathead cabbage offers looser, softer leaves, but Napa cabbage is an ideal substitute.

Why It's Healthy: Utilizing lean skinless chicken thighs provides high-quality amino acids for muscle preservation, while freshly cracked black peppercorns contain piperine, known to enhance nutrient absorption and metabolic thermogenesis.

Ingredients
  • 1 tbsp whole black peppercorns & 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 8 boneless, skinless chicken thighs (~2 lbs) & Salt
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter & 2 tbsp minced shallot
  • 1 cup low-sodium chicken broth & ½ cup heavy cream
  • 3 thyme sprigs, 1 tbsp lemon juice, 1 tbsp chopped parsley
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Crush peppercorns coarsely in a bag with a mallet or saucepan.
  2. Step 2: Heat oil in a 12-inch cast-iron skillet over medium. Season chicken with salt, sear in two batches until golden (~5 mins per batch), and set aside. Pour off remaining oil.
  3. Step 3: Melt butter in skillet, cook shallot for 1 minute, then add broth, cream, thyme, and cracked peppercorns, scraping up pan browned bits.
  4. Step 4: Return chicken to skillet, bring to a simmer, and cook until internal temp reaches 165°F (6–8 mins).
  5. Step 5: Transfer chicken to plates. Stir lemon juice and parsley into the sauce until thickened, spoon over chicken, and serve.
Serving Option: Serve with crusty bread or egg noodles if desired.

Why It's Healthy: Lean chicken provides high-quality protein, while cumin, garlic, cilantro, and lime add flavor without excess fat. Plain Greek yogurt in the green sauce adds protein and tang in place of mayonnaise.

Ingredients
  • 2 lb boneless, skinless chicken breasts or thighs
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • 3–4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
Cilantro-Lime Green Sauce
  • 1 cup fresh cilantro
  • 1 jalapeño, roughly chopped
  • 1 garlic clove
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • ½ cup plain Greek yogurt
  • 1 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • ½ tsp salt
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Marinate the chicken. Mix olive oil, lime juice, garlic, cumin, paprika, oregano, salt and pepper. Coat chicken thoroughly. Marinate 20–30 minutes or longer in the refrigerator.
  2. Step 2: Make the green sauce. Blend cilantro, jalapeño, garlic, lime juice, Greek yogurt, olive oil and salt until smooth. Refrigerate.
  3. Step 3: Roast the chicken. Preheat oven to 425°F. Place chicken on a lightly oiled baking sheet. Roast approximately 18–25 minutes, depending on thickness, until the thickest part reaches 165°F.
  4. Step 4: Rest and serve. Let chicken rest 5 minutes before slicing. Generously spoon green sauce over the chicken.
Recommended Sides: Ginger-turmeric rice cooked in chicken broth; fresh cucumber and tomato salad; grilled zucchini or asparagus. The secret: Don't skimp on the green sauce! The combination of cumin-garlic chicken and cool, tangy cilantro-lime sauce makes this dish special.

Golden, buttery leeks baked in garlic-Dijon cream with Gruyère and Parmesan.
Why It's Healthy: Leeks are a fiber-rich allium vegetable, and the dish leans on garlic, thyme, and Dijon for flavor, with cream and cheese kept to a measured topping rather than the main event.

Ingredients
  • 4 large leeks
  • 1 cup heavy whipping cream
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 garlic clove, finely minced
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • ½ tsp salt, or to taste
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • ½ tsp fresh thyme (or ¼ tsp dried)
  • ¾ cup shredded Gruyère cheese
  • 2 tbsp grated Parmesan cheese
  • Small squeeze of fresh lemon juice
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Prepare the leeks. Cut off roots and dark green tops. Slice the white and light green portions crosswise into ½-inch rounds. Separate the layers and rinse thoroughly to remove dirt. Drain and arrange in a buttered glass baking dish.
  2. Step 2: Make the cream sauce. In a small saucepan, gently warm the heavy cream, butter, garlic, Dijon mustard, salt, pepper and thyme. Stir until combined. Do not boil.
  3. Step 3: Assemble. Pour the cream mixture evenly over the leeks. They should be mostly coated.
  4. Step 4: First bake. Cover the baking dish tightly with foil. Bake at 375°F for 25 minutes.
  5. Step 5: Add cheese. Remove the foil. Sprinkle Gruyère and Parmesan evenly over the top.
  6. Step 6: Finish baking. Bake uncovered for another 15–20 minutes, until the leeks are completely tender and the cheese is golden and bubbling. If needed, bake another 5–10 minutes.
  7. Step 7: Rest and serve. Let rest for 5–10 minutes. Finish with a small squeeze of fresh lemon juice and freshly cracked black pepper.
Serving Notes: Excellent alongside roasted chicken, steak, pork or fish. The leeks become sweet and tender, while the cream, garlic, Dijon and Gruyère create a rich French-style sauce. The secret: Cut the leeks into uniform ½-inch rounds, cover during the initial bake to soften them, then uncover with cheese to create that beautiful golden top.

Lemon, Parmesan & Toasted Pine Nuts.
Why It's Healthy: Fresh arugula is a peppery leafy green, and the dressing is simply good olive oil, lemon, and a little garlic — light, fresh, and letting the greens lead.

Ingredients
  • 4–5 packed cups fresh baby arugula (about 4 oz)
  • ¼ cup shaved Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1–2 tbsp pine nuts, lightly toasted
  • Freshly cracked black pepper
  • Flaky sea salt
Fresh Lemon-Garlic Dressing
  • 2½ tbsp good-quality extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 tbsp freshly squeezed lemon juice
  • ¼ small garlic clove, freshly pressed
  • ½ tsp finely grated fresh lemon zest
  • Freshly cracked black pepper
  • Small pinch of sea salt
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Toast the pine nuts. Place in a dry skillet over medium-low heat for 2–3 minutes, stirring frequently until lightly golden and fragrant. Cool.
  2. Step 2: Make the dressing. Whisk together olive oil, lemon juice, pressed garlic, lemon zest, salt, and pepper. Let sit for 3–5 minutes.
  3. Step 3: Prepare the greens. Make sure arugula is thoroughly washed and dried. Place in a large salad bowl.
  4. Step 4: Dress the salad. Drizzle dressing over the arugula a little at a time. Toss gently until lightly coated.
  5. Step 5: Finish. Add shaved Parmesan and toasted pine nuts. Finish with freshly cracked black pepper and flaky sea salt. Serve immediately.
Chef's Notes: Use a vegetable peeler for beautiful, wide Parmesan shavings. Start with less dressing; arugula should be lightly coated, never soggy. Use just a little garlic so it doesn't overpower the lemon and Parmesan. Good extra-virgin olive oil makes a noticeable difference. A few drops of aged balsamic are optional, but lemon should remain the dominant flavor.

Whole Chicken • Fresh Ginger • Turmeric • Lemon & Herbs. A wholesome, comforting, pressure-cooker chicken soup made with simple, high-quality whole-food ingredients.
Why It's Healthy: A whole chicken gives a richer broth and a variety of tender meat, with fresh ginger, turmeric, lemon, and herbs for flavor from real whole-food ingredients — simple and nourishing.

Chicken & Broth
  • 1 whole air-chilled, pasture-raised chicken (4–5 lb)
  • 8 cups low-sodium chicken broth
  • Kosher salt (approximately 1 tsp per 2 lb chicken for advance seasoning)
Vegetables & Aromatics
  • 1 large yellow onion, quartered
  • 3 carrots, sliced
  • 3 celery stalks, sliced
  • 5 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 1 tbsp freshly grated ginger
Seasonings
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 5–6 sprigs fresh thyme
  • 1 tsp whole black peppercorns
  • ½ tsp turmeric
Rice & Fresh Finish
  • ¾ cup jasmine or long-grain white rice, rinsed
  • ¼ cup fresh parsley, chopped
  • Juice of ½–1 fresh lemon
  • Freshly grated lemon zest (¼–½ tsp per bowl)
  • Fresh green onion, thinly sliced (about 1 tbsp per bowl)
  • Freshly ground black pepper, to taste
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Season the chicken. If time allows, season the chicken with approximately 1 tsp kosher salt per 2 lb. Refrigerate uncovered for several hours or overnight. Otherwise, season just before cooking.
  2. Step 2: Pressure-cook the chicken. Add chicken, onion, carrots, celery, garlic, ginger, bay leaves, thyme, peppercorns, turmeric, and chicken broth to the pressure cooker. Do not add rice, parsley, or lemon yet. Cook on High Pressure for 20 minutes. Allow a 15-minute natural release, then carefully vent the remaining pressure. Important: Check your pressure cooker's minimum liquid and maximum fill requirements. Reduce the broth if necessary to stay below the safe fill line.
  3. Step 3: Shred the chicken. Remove the chicken carefully. Discard skin and bones. Shred the breast, legs, and thighs, and pick any usable meat from the neck if desired. Return the shredded chicken to the broth. Taste and adjust salt.
  4. Step 4: Cook the rice. Stir in the rinsed rice. Cook on High Pressure for 4 minutes, followed by a 10-minute natural release. Carefully vent any remaining pressure.
  5. Step 5: Finish the soup. Stir in fresh chopped parsley and lemon juice. Taste and adjust salt, pepper, and lemon to your liking.
  6. Step 6: Garnish each bowl. Finish each serving with: freshly grated lemon zest; thinly sliced green onion; freshly ground black pepper; a little extra parsley, if desired. Add the fresh garnishes immediately before serving.
My Favorite Cooking Notes: Use a whole chicken for richer broth and a variety of tender meat. Yellow onion is the best choice for classic chicken soup flavor. Skip searing for a clean, bright broth. Keep lemon zest, lemon juice, parsley, and green onion fresh until serving. Rice absorbs broth as it sits. Add a splash of hot broth or water when reheating. Refrigerate leftovers promptly and enjoy within 3–4 days, or freeze for longer storage. The signature finish: Fresh lemon juice + lemon zest + green onion + parsley + black pepper. Simple, nourishing, flavorful, and made entirely with real ingredients.

Traditional Roman Pasta | 25 Minutes | Serves 2–3. A timeless Roman pasta with silky egg-yolk sauce, sharp Pecorino Romano, crispy guanciale, and freshly cracked black pepper. No cream or butter.
Why It's Healthy: A simple Roman pasta built on eggs, Pecorino Romano, guanciale, and black pepper — no cream or butter — with the sauce coming from egg yolks, cheese, and starchy pasta water.

Ingredients
  • 8 oz (225 g) bucatini
  • 4 oz (115 g) guanciale, diced (or pancetta)
  • 4 large egg yolks
  • 1 whole egg
  • ¾ cup finely grated Pecorino Romano
  • 1 tsp freshly cracked black pepper, plus more to taste
  • Salt, for pasta water
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Prepare the egg and cheese sauce. In a mixing bowl, whisk together the egg yolks, whole egg, grated Pecorino Romano, and black pepper until thick and creamy. Set aside.
  2. Step 2: Cook the guanciale. Place diced guanciale in a cold skillet. Turn heat to medium-low and slowly render the fat until the pieces are golden and crispy, approximately 6–10 minutes. Turn off the heat and leave the rendered fat in the pan.
  3. Step 3: Cook the pasta. Bring a large pot of water to a boil. Salt lightly. Cook bucatini until al dente. Reserve at least 1 cup of pasta water before draining.
  4. Step 4: Make the carbonara. Transfer the hot bucatini directly into the skillet with the guanciale and rendered fat. Toss thoroughly. Remove the skillet from the heat and allow it to cool for 30–45 seconds. Add the egg and cheese mixture, tossing continuously. Gradually add reserved pasta water, one tablespoon at a time, until the sauce becomes silky, glossy, and coats every strand. Important: Never add the egg mixture over direct heat. Residual heat cooks the eggs without scrambling them.
  5. Step 5: Serve. Twirl pasta into warm bowls. Distribute the crispy guanciale throughout. Finish with freshly grated Pecorino Romano and another generous crack of black pepper.
Secrets to Excellent Carbonara: Use guanciale: Traditional Italian cured pork with incredible flavor. Pancetta is the next-best substitute. Grate cheese finely: This helps it incorporate smoothly. Save pasta water: Its starch helps emulsify the eggs

Waring 166P Ice Cream Parlor. Classic French-style egg custard.
Why It's Healthy: A classic French-style egg custard made from real whole-food ingredients — fresh fruit, milk, cream, egg yolks, and vanilla — with no chunks, seeds, or fibers in the finished ice cream.

Choose ONE Fruit
  • Peach: 1¼ lb ripe fresh peaches, peeled and pitted.
  • Strawberry: 1¼ lb ripe fresh strawberries, washed and hulled.
Fruit Preparation
  • ¼ cup granulated sugar
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp mild honey
Creamy Custard Base
  • 2 cups whole milk
  • 1½ cups heavy cream
  • 6 large egg yolks
  • ¼ cup granulated sugar
  • ¼ tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Prepare the Fruit. Peel and pit 1¼ lb peaches OR wash and hull 1¼ lb strawberries. Slice all the fruit. Combine in a saucepan with: ¼ cup granulated sugar; 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice; 1 tbsp mild honey. Let macerate for 20 minutes. Important: Do not reserve any fruit pieces. All the fruit will be puréed completely smooth. We want absolutely no chunks in the finished ice cream.
  2. Step 2: Cook and Concentrate the Fruit. Cook the fruit mixture over medium-low heat for approximately 15–20 minutes, stirring frequently. Cook until the fruit is very soft and the mixture is thick, syrupy and noticeably reduced. Allow excess water to evaporate. This is especially important with strawberries, which contain considerable water. Do not aggressively boil or caramelize. Let cool for 10 minutes. Transfer the entire mixture, including its syrup, to a blender. Blend until completely smooth. Pass through a fine-mesh strainer to remove strawberry seeds, peach fibers and any remaining solids. Refrigerate.
  3. Step 3: Make the Custard. Combine in a saucepan: 2 cups whole milk; 1½ cups heavy cream; ¼ cup granulated sugar; ¼ tsp kosher salt. Heat over medium heat until steaming. Do not boil. In a separate bowl, whisk 6 large egg yolks. Slowly whisk 1 cup of the hot dairy mixture into the egg yolks. Pour the tempered egg mixture back into the saucepan with the remaining 2½ cups dairy. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, until the custard reaches approximately 175°F and coats the back of a spoon. Do not boil.
  4. Step 4: Strain and Combine. Pour the custard through a fine-mesh strainer into a clean bowl. Add: All of the cooked, puréed and strained fruit mixture; 1 tsp pure vanilla extract. Whisk thoroughly until completely uniform. The finished base should be entirely smooth, with no fruit chunks, seeds or fibers.
  5. Step 5: Chill Thoroughly. Place the bowl over an ice bath and stir until completely cool. Cover and refrigerate for at least 6 hours, preferably overnight. The mixture must be thoroughly cold before churning. This helps the Waring freeze the mixture faster, producing smaller ice crystals.
  6. Step 6: Churn in the Waring 166P. Pour the thoroughly chilled mixture into the freezing canister. Do not exceed the maximum fill level. Install the dasher and lid. Prepare the ice-and-salt freezing bath according to the Waring 166P instructions. Churn approximately 20–30 minutes, or until thick and creamy, similar to firm soft serve. Do not rely exclusively on the clock. Watch the consistency.
  7. Step 7: Harden. Immediately transfer the finished ice cream to a shallow, airtight freezer container. Smooth the surface. Press parchment paper or plastic wrap directly against the ice cream. Seal and freeze for at least 4 hours, preferably overnight. Keep the container toward the back of the freezer, where the temperature is most stable. Let stand for approximately 5–10 minutes before scooping.

Fresh shrimp, garden cucumber, herbs, rice noodles & homemade peanut dipping sauce.
Why It's Healthy: Fresh shrimp, garden cucumber, carrots, and lots of fresh herbs with rice noodles — light and fresh, with a homemade peanut sauce you control.

Summer Rolls
  • 1 lb medium or large shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 8–10 rice paper wrappers (8–9 inch)
  • 3–4 oz dry rice vermicelli noodles
  • 1–2 cucumbers, cut into thin matchsticks
  • 2 carrots, peeled and cut into thin matchsticks
  • 1 head butter lettuce or green leaf lettuce
  • ½ cup fresh basil leaves
  • ½ cup fresh mint leaves
  • ½ cup fresh cilantro leaves
  • 3 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive or avocado oil
  • Salt and black pepper
Homemade Peanut Dipping Sauce
  • ⅓ cup creamy peanut butter, preferably unsweetened
  • ¼ cup hoisin sauce
  • ½ cup warm water
  • 1 tbsp fresh lime juice
  • 1 tsp rice vinegar
  • 1 garlic clove, finely grated
  • 1–2 tsp Sriracha, optional
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Prepare the Peanut Sauce.
    1. Combine ⅓ cup peanut butter, ¼ cup hoisin, 1 tbsp lime juice, 1 tsp rice vinegar, 1 grated garlic clove and 1–2 tsp Sriracha.
    2. Gradually whisk in ½ cup warm water until smooth.
    3. Adjust consistency with additional water if needed.
    4. Refrigerate until ready to serve.
  2. Step 2: Cook the Shrimp.
    1. Pat 1 lb shrimp dry.
    2. Season lightly with salt and black pepper.
    3. Heat 1 tbsp olive or avocado oil in a skillet over medium-high heat.
    4. Cook shrimp approximately 1–2 minutes per side, until opaque and cooked through (145°F internal temperature).
    5. Allow to cool.
    6. Slice shrimp in half lengthwise.
  3. Step 3: Prepare the Noodles.
    1. Cook 3–4 oz rice vermicelli according to package instructions.
    2. Drain and rinse thoroughly under cold water.
    3. Drain very well and set aside.
  4. Step 4: Prepare the Vegetables & Herbs.
    1. Cut cucumbers and 2 carrots into thin matchsticks.
    2. Thinly slice 3 scallions.
    3. Wash and thoroughly dry lettuce, basil, mint and cilantro.
    4. Separate lettuce leaves into manageable pieces.
  5. Step 5: Assemble the Rolls.
    1. Fill a large shallow dish with room-temperature water.
    2. Dip one rice paper wrapper into the water for approximately 5–10 seconds. Remove while still slightly firm; it will continue softening.
    3. Lay wrapper flat on a clean, damp cutting board.
    4. Place a piece of lettuce near the bottom third.
    5. Add a small handful of rice noodles.
    6. Add cucumber, carrot and scallions.
    7. Add generous amounts of basil, mint and cilantro.
    8. Place 3–4 shrimp halves across the filling.
  6. Step 6: Roll.
    1. Fold the bottom edge over the filling.
    2. Fold both sides inward.
    3. Roll forward tightly, like a burrito.
    4. Place seam-side down.
    5. Repeat with remaining ingredients.
Serving: Serve immediately with homemade peanut dipping sauce and fresh lime wedges. For best results, serve fresh. If making ahead, wrap each roll individually in plastic wrap and refrigerate for up to one day. Avoid stacking.
Variations:
  1. Classic Shrimp & Pork — Add thinly sliced cooked pork tenderloin alongside the shrimp. A traditional Vietnamese combination.
  2. Avocado Summer Rolls — Add thin slices of avocado for extra creaminess and healthy fats.
  3. Spicy Summer Rolls — Add thinly sliced jalapeño and extra Sriracha to the peanut sauce.
  4. Chicken Summer Rolls — Substitute grilled or poached chicken breast for shrimp.
  5. Extra Vegetable Rolls — Add bean sprouts, shredded cabbage, daikon radish or bell pepper.
My Favorite Combination: Shrimp + garden cucumber + carrot + rice noodles + butter lettuce + lots of fresh basil, mint and cilantro + homemade peanut sauce. Cooking tip: Don't overstuff the rolls, don't oversoak the rice paper, and dry the vegetables and noodles thoroughly. These three things make the biggest difference in getting beautiful, tightly rolled summer rolls.

A simple, classic baked potato with extra-crispy, salty skin and a light, fluffy interior.
Why It's Healthy: A simple, classic baked potato with extra-crispy, salty skin and a light, fluffy interior — just a russet, olive oil, salt, and fresh toppings.

Ingredients
  • 1 large russet potato
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Kosher salt
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Butter
  • Sharp cheddar cheese, freshly grated
  • Sour cream
  • Fresh green onion or garlic chives, finely chopped
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Prepare the potato. Preheat oven to 350°F. Scrub the russet potato thoroughly and dry completely. Pierce 4–6 times with a fork.
  2. Step 2: Season the skin. Rub the entire potato generously with olive oil. Coat all sides with kosher salt.
  3. Step 3: First bake. Place directly on the oven rack, with a baking sheet underneath to catch drippings. Bake at 350°F for 80 minutes.
  4. Step 4: Cool. Remove from the oven and let the potato cool for approximately 20–30 minutes. This allows steam to escape and helps develop a better texture.
  5. Step 5: Second bake. Return the potato directly to the oven rack and bake at 350°F for another 20 minutes. The skin should be nicely crisp and the inside soft and fluffy.
  6. Step 6: Open and fluff. Remove from the oven. Cut a lengthwise slit across the top and gently squeeze the ends to open. Fluff the interior with a fork.
  7. Step 7: Add toppings. While piping hot, add butter, freshly grated sharp cheddar, salt, and freshly ground black pepper. Let the cheese melt.
  8. Step 8: Finish. Top with a generous spoonful of sour cream and finely chopped fresh green onion or garlic chives.
The finishing touch: Serve immediately while the skin is crispy, the center is steaming hot, and the cheddar is melted. Don't wrap in foil at any point. The key: Olive oil, plenty of kosher salt, baking uncovered, and allowing the potato to cool between the two bakes.

Why It's Healthy: Potatoes layered with butter, half-and-half, and grated Parmesan or Gruyère — simple, whole-food ingredients.

Ingredients
  • 3 to 4 pounds potatoes, peeled and cut into ¼-inch slices
  • Kosher salt and black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 2 to 3 cups half-and-half or heavy cream
  • ¼ cup grated Parmesan or Gruyère
  • Pinch of nutmeg
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Heat oven to 400 degrees. Seasoning each layer with salt and pepper as you go, layer the potatoes in a 10- or 12-inch ovenproof skillet.
  2. Step 2: Dot the top with 2 tablespoons butter. Pour in half-and-half. (It should come about ¾ of the way to the top.)
  3. Step 3: Bring to a boil over medium-high heat. Reduce to low and cook for 10 minutes, uncovered, until the half-and-half has reduced by about half.
  4. Step 4: Transfer to the oven and bake until the top is golden brown, about 10 minutes. Reduce heat to 300 degrees and cook until the potatoes are fork tender and the top is nicely browned, about 8 minutes more. Top with grated cheese and return to the oven until the cheese is melty and golden brown, about 3 to 5 minutes. Sprinkle with a pinch of nutmeg, if using, and serve.
Yield: 6 to 8 servings | Oven: 300 degrees, then 400 degrees

Why It's Healthy: Pork ribs braised low and slow in pineapple juice, soy, and vinegar, finished with a homemade ketchup-honey glaze — simple, whole-food ingredients.

For the Ribs
  • 1 tablespoon garlic powder
  • 1 tablespoon onion powder
  • 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt (such as Diamond Crystal)
  • ½ teaspoon black pepper
  • 3 to 4 pounds St. Louis- or country-style pork ribs, cut into individual ribs (see Tips)
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable or canola oil for searing, plus more if necessary
  • 1 medium onion, sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 1 cup pure pineapple juice
  • ⅓ cup soy sauce
  • 2 tablespoons apple cider vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
For the Glaze
  • ½ cup ketchup
  • ¼ cup honey
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons (light or dark) brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon unsalted butter
For serving
  • Cooked white rice, cornbread or coleslaw, optional.
Preparation
  1. Step 1: Prepare the ribs. Heat the oven to 300 degrees. In a small bowl, stir together the garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, salt and pepper. Pat the ribs dry with paper towels and rub the seasoning evenly over every side.
  2. Step 2: Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high until shimmering. Working in batches so the pot is not crowded and lowering the heat as necessary to avoid scorching, sear the ribs on all sides until deeply browned, 2 to 3 minutes per side. Transfer to a plate as they finish.
  3. Step 3: Reduce the heat to medium. Add the onion and garlic to the pot and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and just beginning to color, 2 to 3 minutes. Pour in the pineapple juice, soy sauce, vinegar and Worcestershire sauce, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot.
  4. Step 4: Return the ribs and any accumulated juices to the pot, nestling them into the liquid — it should come about halfway up the ribs. Cover and transfer to the oven. Bake for 1 hour, remove the ribs from the oven and gently toss and rearrange. Return to oven and cook another 45 minutes to 1 hour, until very tender but not falling off the bone. Remove the Dutch oven from the oven and uncover it. Raise the oven temperature to 400 degrees.
  5. Step 5: While the oven heats, prepare the glaze. Combine the ketchup, honey, brown sugar and butter in a small saucepan. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, reduce heat to medium-low and cook, stirring occasionally, for about 5 minutes, until the glaze is thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
  6. Step 6: Transfer the ribs to a rack set on a baking sheet. Pour or brush the glaze over the ribs, turning each piece to coat. Return the ribs to the oven and cook for 5 to 7 minutes, until caramelized. If you'd like, skim the fat from the braising liquid in the pot. Serve the ribs hot, with the braising liquid spooned over rice or reduced into a sauce on the side.
Tips: Country-style ribs or a St. Louis cut both work. Baby backs cook a touch faster — start checking at the 1 hour 30 minute mark. Ask the butcher to cut the rack into individual ribs, or do it yourself with a sharp chef's knife between the bones before seasoning.
