Step 0 — Universe (mine)

US-listed common stocks. Market cap $1B+; price $10+; average volume 500K+ shares. Exclude financials and utilities from the ROIC/EBIT steps; screen banks and insurers separately on P/B and ROE.

Step 1A — Value + quality screen

Cheap (Greenblatt, Graham, Lynch): earnings yield (EBIT / EV) 8%+ — Greenblatt ranks; 8% is my cutoff. P/E 20 or less (strict Graham mode: 15 or less). P/B 3 or less (strict Graham: 1.5 or less; P/E × P/B 22.5 or less). PEG 1 or less (Lynch); under 0.5 is a standout.

Quality (Buffett, Smith, Greenblatt): ROIC 15%+ (Smith ROCE tier: 20%+). ROE 15%+. Debt/equity 0.5 or less (mine); net debt / EBITDA 2 or less (mine). Free cash flow / net income 90%+ (Smith “cash conversion”). Gross margin 40%+ (mine; Smith favors high margins). FCF yield 4%+ (mine). Positive earnings every year for the last 5 years (Graham uses 10).

Health: Piotroski F-score 7+ (mine — Piotroski’s own “strong” line is 8–9).

Step 1B — Growth + momentum screen (alternative to 1A)

Growth (O’Neil): current quarter EPS growth vs. year ago 25%+; annual EPS growth, 3-year, 25%+; sales growth, current quarter, 20%+ and accelerating (mine on the number); ROE 17%+.

Trend Template (Minervini): price above the 150-day and 200-day MA; 150-day MA above the 200-day MA; 200-day MA rising for 1+ months; 50-day MA above the 150-day and 200-day; price above the 50-day MA; price 30%+ above the 52-week low; price within 25% of the 52-week high; relative strength top 20% of the universe by 12-month return, excluding the last month (proxy for the IBD RS rating of 80+).

Step 2 — Rank the survivors

Sum the ranks (1 = best) for: earnings yield, ROIC, FCF yield, 12-month relative strength. Take the top 20–30 (Greenblatt style) or top 10 for a concentrated list. Don’t blend 1A and 1B blindly; cheap stocks often fail the trend rules. Pick one lead style, use the other as confirmation.

Step 3 — Research before buying

Read the last 10-K and last earnings call. Why is it cheap or strong? Is the earnings drop or jump one-time? Check insider activity and share count trend (buybacks or dilution). Check debt maturity and cash flow. Estimate intrinsic value; buy only with a 25–50% margin of safety (Buffett/Klarman).

Step 4 — Risk rules

Entry for momentum names: breakout from a tight base on rising volume (Minervini). Stop loss: 7–8% below entry for momentum trades (Minervini); for value, use thesis-based exits (mine). Risk 0.5–1% of the portfolio per position (mine; Minervini uses more). Average loss should be well below the average gain. Rebalance value picks yearly (Greenblatt, O’Shaughnessy).

Step 5 — Benchmark (Bogle)

Track results vs. a total-market index fund after taxes and fees. If you can’t beat it over 3–5 years, shift core money to the index. John Bogle’s Cost Matters Hypothesis: after costs, the average active investor must underperform a low-cost index fund by exactly the amount of the costs. SPIVA Year-End 2025: 89.5% of US large-cap funds underperformed after costs over 15 years; over 20 years, ~93–94% fell short. Buffett’s 2007–2017 bet proved the point: a Vanguard S&P 500 index fund compounded 7.1%/yr against hedge funds, after fees.
