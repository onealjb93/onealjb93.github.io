💡 Peter Lynch's Growth at a Reasonable Price (GARP)

Peter Lynch sought out fast-growing businesses without paying inflated bubble prices. When you turn the filter ON, it screens every stock in the table against these 4 strict numerical rules:

1. PEG Ratio (Found in table column: "PEG Ratio")

Measures stock valuation relative to its earnings growth. Calculated as P/E divided by growth rate.

Ideal: ≤ 1.0 (Bargain) Overpriced: > 1.5
2. Earnings Growth (Found in table column: "Earnings Growth")

The annual percentage rate at which the company's net income is expanding.

Ideal: 20% – 50% (Sweet Spot) Trap/Too High: > 50%
3. Debt-to-Equity (Found in table column: "Debt-to-Equity")

Compares total company liabilities to shareholder equity. Shows how heavily leveraged the business is.

Ideal: ≤ 0.30 (Secure) Risky: > 0.60
4. Institutional Ownership (Found in table column: "Institutional Ownership")

The percentage of stock owned by large financial institutions (mutual funds, banks, pension funds).

Ideal: < 50% (Hidden Gem) Crowded: > 75%

🪄 Joel Greenblatt's Magic Formula (Objective Ranking Model)

A mathematical ranking model designed to buy good companies (high return on capital) at bargain prices (high earnings yield) without emotion:

1. Earnings Yield (Cheapness)

Operating earnings divided by enterprise value. Tells you cash return relative to what you pay.

Ideal: Top Decile (Highest Yield) Unfavorable: Bottom Decile
2. Return on Capital (Quality)

Pre-tax operating profit relative to fixed assets and working capital. Measures how efficiently capital is used.

Ideal: Top Decile (High Efficiency) Unfavorable: Low Return
3. Combined Score Rank

The sum of the company's Yield rank and Capital Return rank across the stock universe. Lower is better.

Ideal: Ranks 1 to 6 (Top Buys) Unfavorable: Ranks > 15
4. Portfolio Discipline

Mechanical execution: buy top-ranked compounders, hold for exactly 1 year, sell, and repeat.

Action: Buy Top 20–30, Hold 1 Year
